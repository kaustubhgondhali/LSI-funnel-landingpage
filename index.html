Why Building Wealth Often Feels Harder Than It Should

These are some of the reasons people commonly mention. They may not apply to everyone, and recognising them is usually the first useful step.

Starting Too Late

Postponing a financial plan may reduce the time available for compounding to work.

Irregular Saving Habits

Saving only when money remains at the end of the month can be inconsistent.

The first reveal

The First Step Is Not a Big Investment. It Is a Clear, Consistent Plan.

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows an eligible investor to invest a chosen amount at regular intervals into a selected mutual fund scheme, instead of trying to invest a large sum at the right moment.

  1. 1

    Define the financial goal

    Be specific about what the money is for and roughly when it will be needed.

  2. 2

    Determine a suitable monthly amount

    Choose an amount you can realistically continue with, month after month.

  3. 3

    Understand your risk profile and horizon

    How long you can stay invested, and how much fluctuation you can tolerate, both matter.

  4. 4

    Select an appropriate scheme

    Review the scheme's risks, category and features before deciding.

  5. 5

    Stay disciplined and review

    Maintain the plan and revisit it periodically as circumstances change.

Why time matters

Give Your Investments Time to Grow.

When returns stay invested rather than being withdrawn, any growth that is earned can itself go on to generate further growth. That effect tends to be more visible over longer periods. With mutual funds, returns are market-linked — they are not fixed and not guaranteed, and the value of an investment can fall as well as rise.

The process

What the SIP Journey Usually Looks Like

  1. 1

    Define your financial goals

    Name the goal and the approximate timeline attached to it.

  2. 2

    Review your budget, horizon and risk profile

    Work out a monthly amount you can sustain, and how much fluctuation you are comfortable with.

  3. 3

    Learn about suitable fund categories and schemes

    Different categories carry different risks. Understanding them comes before choosing.

  4. 4

    Set up a suitable SIP

    Read the scheme information document and risk factors, complete KYC, and start only once the scheme is understood.

  5. 5

    Review periodically and adjust

    Revisit the plan as income, goals or timelines change.

Investment choices should be based on your own circumstances, objectives and risk tolerance. Consider consulting a qualified professional before investing.
